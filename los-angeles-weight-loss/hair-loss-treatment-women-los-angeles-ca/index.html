    How Women's Hair Loss Treatment Works in Los Angeles, CA

    How Women's Hair Loss Treatment Works in Los Angeles

    Female pattern hair loss widens the part and thins the top while the front hairline usually stays put. Inherited sensitivity to androgens gradually shrinks follicles on the crown and part. Minoxidil helps follicles stay in the growth phase longer. Off-label spironolactone dampens androgen effects on hair.

    Who Women's Treatment Is For

    It suits adult women with gradual thinning, a widening part or less volume on top. Women who noticed shedding two to four months after childbirth, surgery, illness or a big stress may also benefit from a review, though that type often settles on its own within six months. Patchy loss is different. Bald spots or a painful scalp call for a dermatologist visit. Your photos help sort it out.

    Dosing and Schedule in Los Angeles

    Topical minoxidil is FDA-approved for women as a 5 percent foam once a day or a 2 percent solution twice a day. Low-dose oral minoxidil is an off-label daily pill, often started around 0.625 to 1.25 mg, for women who dislike applying something to their scalp. Spironolactone starts low, often 25 to 50 mg, and is raised slowly. Topical blends are applied daily.

    What to Expect Month by Month

    Expect some extra shedding in months one and two. It is usually temporary, and it happens because minoxidil pushes resting hairs out so new growing hairs can replace them, which feels backward but is a normal sign. By months three to six, shedding slows and new growth may appear along the part. Fuller results show near month twelve.

    Side Effects of Women's Treatment in Los Angeles

    Scalp itch, dryness and fine facial hair, if the liquid drips onto the forehead or cheeks, are the usual topical minoxidil complaints. Low-dose oral minoxidil sometimes causes unwanted hair growth or fluid retention. Spotting and breast soreness are common with spironolactone. Potassium may rise on it. Tell your provider about a racing heart.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Pregnancy, breastfeeding and plans to conceive come first in the review. Spironolactone and DHT blockers can affect a developing fetus, so your provider confirms reliable contraception before prescribing either, and oral minoxidil is avoided during pregnancy too. Blood pressure and kidney health are reviewed. Labs for thyroid, ferritin and vitamin D may be suggested.

    How Women's Options Compare in Los Angeles

    Minoxidil is the base of most plans and the main FDA-approved option for women. Spironolactone is a common off-label add-on for women whose thinning seems hormone-related, especially when it comes with adult acne, irregular cycles or PCOS. The minoxidil and GHK-Cu blend is compounded and lacks FDA approval. Finasteride is mostly kept for after menopause.
